<h2 id="values">Our Values</h2>

Redwoods has three core values that provide the foundation of every interaction we have. These are
actual, not aspirational. If you're a member, this is who we are, not who we want to be. We're not
perfect, so we won't always get this right. But we are on a mission to be better humans and better
practitioners.

### Stay in learning mode.

Everyone, from junior to senior, has something to teach you. Redwoods encourages continuous and open
learning, across experience level and discipline.

### Be generous with your knowledge.

Learning is great, but it's not enough. Redwoods members are creators, not just consumers: we
analyze, we synthesize, and then we offer our new understanding back to the larger community.

### We're in this together.

Respect is our default. Redwoods is a safe and open community where everyone's experiences and
perspectives are valued. We're here to support, advise, and guide you throughout your systems career.

## Why Redwoods?

Companies don't know how to support their design system teams. We're often left on an island, and
the result is design systems that never generate the value we hoped for.

We believe deeply in what a design system can do, but systems have to be nurtured. And the way you
nurture a system is by nurturing the people building it.

Redwoods is a committed community for the people doing this work. A safe place to talk about the
real challenges, support each other through them, and start producing the results we've been
promising our companies for years.

<Quote cite="Jules" role="Redwoods member">
  “[Redwoods is] your design system team's design system team.”
</Quote>

And because we're makers, membership is also a commitment to give back, to synthesize what we're
learning and share it with the wider design system community.

## What membership is like

Redwoods runs on a simple rhythm. Here are the ways to show up.

### Daily

We use Slack to hang out, catch up, and connect.

### Weekly

- **Standup:** early each week, share a success, a challenge, and what you're working on in
  #weekly-standup.
- **Weekly call:** a one-hour video call every Tuesday at noon Eastern. Come to as many as you like,
  and volunteer to facilitate or demo. As we grow we'll add calls and adjust for other time zones.

### Monthly

- **All-Redwoods call:** first Friday of each month at noon Eastern. Hear what's happening, meet new
  members, and find ways to get more involved.
- **Action channels:** each month we run #action- channels for people who want to build something
  together. Every one has a goal, a ~30-day deadline, and an owner. These channels co-publish
  learnings, build tools and templates, create talks, and find other ways to share what we synthesize
  back with the community.

### Quarterly

- **The Redwoods Quarterly:** we occasionally product an online design system event covering the
  recent shifts in the DS space. It gives us something to work toward and a stage to share our
  biggest takeaways. A subset of Redwoods membership presents and everyone else attends free.

### Also included

- Discount codes for industry events and online courses.
- A Collab Figma seat as part of your membership.
- Behind-the-scenes access to [The Question](https://bencallahan.com/the-question), a live design
  system research show.
- Discounts on individual coaching and strategy sessions.

## Is Redwoods for you?

Redwoods is structured to prioritize anyone who:

- Aligns with our values
- Wants to grow in their craft
- Wants to make a sustainable impact on the industry

We have researchers, product managers, designers, ux-ers, developers...you name it. If you're a
part-time design system team of one, or one of 30 systems practitioners working at enterprise scale,
Redwoods has a place for you.

Consultants and folks at product companies are welcome too, just know this is a space for mutual
support, not sales.

## The history of Redwoods

I was in Muir Woods a few years ago, a coastal redwood forest, and a genuinely spiritual place to
visit. The ground is covered in needles, there's no noise, the air is pure, and you're surrounded by
trees as tall as the Statue of Liberty. It's breathtaking.

When people first settled the area, they cut these trees down for lumber but would leave a few
standing. Inevitably, after a short time, the trees left behind would fall over.

Even though these trees are hundreds of feet tall, their roots only go down about ten feet. But
those roots grow outward, as much as 80 feet, and intertwine with the roots of every other redwood
around them. These trees hold each other up. That's what Redwoods is. A place to hold each other up.

Beyond this, if you read the writings of John Muir, you'll recognize that he saw the
interconnectedness present in a Redwood forest:

<Quote cite="John Muir" marker="―">
  “When one tugs at a single thing in nature, he finds it attached to the rest of the world.”
</Quote>

I started Redwoods because I needed that connection. If you're looking for it too, perhaps Redwoods
is for you.

Ben Callahan,<br />Founder of Redwoods
