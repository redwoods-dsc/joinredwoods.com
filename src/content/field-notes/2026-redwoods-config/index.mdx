_Ahead of Config, we knew that a lot of our members would already be out in SF for Figma's big event. Many of us had attended in the past, but we hadn't really made an effort to connect while previously there. So, why not be more intentional this year?_

### The backdrop

With the support of three sponsors: [Southleft](https://bit.ly/4desXmp), [Sparkbox](https://bit.ly/43njpAf), and the [AI & Design Systems Course](https://bit.ly/4wi5FEZ), we were able to rent two large buses and take over 50 design system nerds out to [Muir Woods](https://bit.ly/4eCyIwk). On our way back to town, we stopped to hang out and have some drinks. It was a fantastic start to Config.

It's been a few months now, and we've asked some of the members who joined us to share a bit about what the event was like for them.

### Stories

<Quote cite="TJ Pitre" role=" Product Architect, AI & Design Systems at Figma, Founder of Southleft" link="https://www.linkedin.com/in/tpitre/">
    "I wasn't able to make the hike itself, but the Beer Garden happy hour after was one of my favorite parts of Config. I got to catch up in person with people I'd only known through the Redwoods Slack and various design systems community groups, some I hadn't seen in a while, some I was meeting face-to-face for the first time. There's something different about nerding out on design systems in person versus over Slack threads.

    Southleft was proud to help sponsor the hike this year, alongside the AI and Design Systems course I run with Brad and Ian Frost. Getting to help fund the trail transportation and the happy hour felt like an easy yes. Redwoods is the kind of community that doesn't really have an equivalent elsewhere in this space, and I'm glad it exists."

</Quote>

<br />

<Quote cite="ToniAnn Drenckhahn" role="Senior Manager Design Systems at Etsy" link="https://www.linkedin.com/in/tonianndrenckhahn/">
    “Design Systems work can feel like an energizing niche or an isolating mess of entanglements depending on several factors, but a big one is about the people that surround you. I feel similarly about design conferences sometimes too. During Config, I got to connect with lots Redwoods members in person and spend time exploring Muir Woods with a group of them. The experience of connecting on a human level, and getting to nerd out about the latest in design systems content presented at the conference left me in the energizing niche space, and the hike was a beautiful way to start my week.

    Two years ago I went to Config without this community but my experience felt closer to that isolating mess. I connected with people, but without shared values or skillsets it stayed surface level and even felt a little lonely. Having the Redwoods crew to connect with meant I got to talk systems, life, leadership, and compare notes on the conference with people who think like me and do the work I do.

    The time together left me excited to keep showing up to more of these kinds of things, and really lucky to have people to connect with in an industry that feels volatile and full of AI-shaped question marks right now.”

</Quote>

<Quote cite="Greg Johnson" role="Redwoods Member" link="https://www.linkedin.com/in/gregjdc/">
  "I joined just two months before my second Config. Leading up to the conference, I discovered a
  few other folks from Redwoods would be in attendance and I was excited to connect with them in
  person. Even knowing Redwoods folks would be there, I wasn't prepared for how much that would
  change the experience. I signed up for the Redwoods hike at Muir Woods, which turned out to be a
  great way to meet other design systems practitioners too. Every day I was able to find people to
  hang out with, people to sit with at the best talks like 'Design Systems Anarchy by Redwoods' own
  Lauren LoPrete, and grab a bite or some drinks with fellow design systems nerds. I made some great
  connections and shared some great memories. Redwoods made all the difference. Not just in having a
  good conference, but in feeling like I belonged somewhere."
</Quote>

<br />

<Quote cite="Katie Orzek" role="Sr. Art Director, Digital Product, Fast Company + Inc." link="">
    "What first drew me into Redwoods was actually a chance to get into the woods and snag another stamp in my National Parks & Monuments Passport booklet. But I quickly gained so much more. Having emailed with Ben a little bit before the Muir Woods meetup at Config, I had a feeling I was going to be surrounded by good people. And I was right.

    I immediately felt welcomed with the group. Although I started the hike doing my own thing, I quickly opened up with members hiking with me and started getting to know some of them better. I met Lauren LePrete and heard how she prepped for her talk on the Config stage she would have a couple days later. I got to connect with DS people from publishing, discovering they've faced similar challenges I currently face myself.

    I am a designer who is responsible for building our baseline site system from the ground up and I'm the only team member well educated in this space. It's been lonely at times, and since I've become a member of Redwoods, it's been so nice connecting and talking with people who know exactly what I am experiencing."

</Quote>

<br />

<Quote cite="Lauren LoPrete" role="" link="">
  "Redwoods and friends spent a day hiking in Muir woods together before Config, and the whole thing
  was magical in a way that typical conference connections rarely are. There's something about being
  outside, among massive trees, that strips away the usual performance of networking. People get to
  be real with each other, and connect genuinely. I talked with longtime Redwoods friends and brand
  new faces, and with people early in their career, which I was so grateful for. The event provided
  time to hear about others’ lives and wasn't focused on just the systems they're building. Design
  systems work is community work by nature, even when the job doesn't feel like it day to day.
  Redwoods gets that the community matters as much as the output. We often spend so much time on an
  island inside our own organizations, and this is the place where the challenges and opportunities
  are deeply shared by the community around you."
</Quote>
